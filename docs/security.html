Security & privacy

Life is a single-user app on a computer you control. The design goal: nothing leaves unless you chose it, and what does leave is small and named.

Sign-in

  • Password hashed with PBKDF2-HMAC-SHA256, 600,000 rounds, per-password salt (older hashes upgrade automatically at the next sign-in). Login attempts are rate-limited.
  • Face ID (passkeys / WebAuthn): the server checks the challenge it issued, the origin, the app identity, that the device verified you, and the ECDSA signature. Replays and forged requests are refused (tested).
  • With "Only open Life with Face ID" on, a session also needs a recent Face ID unlock, and the password stops working from the phone.
  • Session cookie: HttpOnly, SameSite=Lax, Secure over HTTPS. Every change-making request needs a CSRF token.

Your secrets

  • Keys you enter (OpenAI, Telegram, Gmail, Garmin) are stored encrypted (Fernet) and shown as "set"; they are never sent back to the browser, and are redacted from error messages and the health page.
  • Passport and ID numbers, health values and file scans are encrypted at rest. Garmin uses saved login tokens, not your password.
  • The repository contains no keys, tokens or passwords: .env, data/ and key files are git-ignored, and a test scans every file for key-shaped strings.

Private links

  • The share Shortcut uses a write-only token: it can add things and read nothing. Calendar and Siri links are separate, read-only, unguessable, show only titles and dates, and can be reset any time. All are rate-limited.

The web side

  • Strict Content-Security-Policy (script-src 'self'), X-Frame-Options: DENY, nosniff, a Permissions-Policy, COOP, and HSTS when served over HTTPS.
  • No innerHTML: the interface is built from DOM nodes, so note text cannot inject markup. Uploads are type- and size-limited. Fetching a link only reaches public addresses (no internal network).
  • No analytics, no third-party scripts, fonts or trackers. Map tiles are the only third-party request, and only when you open a map.

Honest limits

  • Entry rules and costs are helpers, not advice: always confirm with the embassy or official site before you book.
  • Prices are estimates; areas and map pins are only filled when a post states them or OpenStreetMap knows the place.
  • Entry rules come from an airline's public visa web service that Life calls unofficially (a handful of requests per country, cached for a week, with polite spacing). It may change or block this at any time, and Life then simply shows nothing rather than guessing.
  • Instagram can hide captions or change its pages; Life then falls back to reading the slides, and finally to your Inbox.
  • Garmin's library is unofficial and may break; file import keeps working.
